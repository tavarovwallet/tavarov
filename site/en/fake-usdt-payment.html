How to spot a fake USDT payment

Edited screenshots, “flash USDT” and links to look-alike explorer pages. Here is how these tricks work, and how to check any USDT payment yourself in about 30 seconds.

Updated 14 November 2026

The short version. A payment is real only when you see it yourself: in your own wallet or on an explorer you opened yourself, in the real USDT token, confirmed, and your balance went up. A screenshot, a link from the buyer, or a token that is only called “USDT” proves nothing.

How fake USDT payments work

1. An edited screenshot

A screenshot is a picture. It can be edited. It can also show someone else’s real payment to a different wallet. Either way, it tells you nothing about your own wallet.

2. A token that is only named “USDT”

On BNB Chain and other networks, anyone can create a token and give it any name and logo, including “USDT” or “Tether USD”. These are often sold as “flash USDT”. Such a token can show up in your wallet and on the explorer as an incoming transfer.

But it is a different token, with a different contract address. It has no value: you can’t sell it or spend it. Some are even advertised as disappearing after a while. Real USDT has no “flash” version.

3. A link to a look-alike page

The buyer sends a link “to the transaction”. The page looks like a blockchain explorer, but it is a copy on a different web address. Or it is a real explorer page that shows a transfer to someone else, or in a fake token.

How to check a payment in 30 seconds

For USDT on BNB Chain. Do it every time, before you hand over the goods.

  1. Open it yourself. Use your own wallet app, or type bscscan.com into your browser. Never use a link, QR code or screenshot from the buyer.
  2. Find the incoming transfer. On BscScan, search for your own wallet address and open the tab with token transfers.
  3. Check the token contract, not the name. Open the token. Its contract address must be exactly this: 0x55d398326f99059fF775485246999027B3197955 Compare the first characters (0x55d398) and the last ones (3197955). Any other address is not USDT, whatever it is called. Some explorers label the real one “Binance-Peg BSC-USD”; that is normal for USDT on BNB Chain.
  4. Check the amount. Exactly what you asked for, sent to your address.
  5. Check the status. It must say “Success”. On BNB Chain a transfer is confirmed within seconds. “Pending” is not money yet.
  6. Check your balance. Your USDT balance went up by that amount. If it didn’t, you have not been paid.

Other networks. The steps are the same on Ethereum, Tron, Solana and others, with that network’s explorer. Look up the official USDT contract for that network yourself (Tether publishes its list at tether.to) and compare it the same way.

Red flags

  • They rush you: “quick, I’m in a hurry”.
  • They ask you to “check the screenshot” instead of your wallet.
  • “It’s pending, just release the goods.”
  • They send more than the price, then ask you to send back the difference.
  • The “USDT” in your wallet shows no price, a $0 value, or a warning.
  • They send you a link to “see the transaction”.

See one of these? Stop, and check it yourself with the steps above.

Questions

What is flash USDT?

A token that copies the name and often the logo of USDT but has a different contract address. It has no value. Real USDT has no “flash” version, so any offer to sell or send “flash USDT” is a scam.

Can a fake USDT end up in my wallet?

Yes. Anyone can send any token to any address. Its arrival does not mean you were paid. Leave it alone: don’t try to swap it and don’t open websites named in it.

Is a screenshot proof of payment?

No. A screenshot can be edited or show someone else’s payment. Check your own wallet or an explorer you opened yourself.

What is the USDT contract address on BNB Chain?

0x55d398326f99059fF775485246999027B3197955. Check it yourself on bscscan.com, and compare the first and last characters every time.
